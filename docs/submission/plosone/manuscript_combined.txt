TITLE
========================================================================


Conserved geometry of cell-type identity across mammals, and a resolution limit on per-type divergence

Short title: Conserved cell-type geometry across mammals and the limits of per-type divergence

Authors:
Sriram Devadas*
Independent Researcher, Acton, MA, USA

*Corresponding author: sriram@sriramdevadas.com


========================================================================
Abstract
========================================================================


The relative positions of cell types in transcriptomic space, the geometry of cell-type identity, are widely assumed to be conserved across species. But which features of that geometry are conserved, and which can be measured reliably in current data, have not been separated. We represent each cell type as a landmark, its mean expression across orthologous genes, and use Procrustes superimposition to decompose the cross-species geometry into two levels: the configuration of cell types relative to one another (Layer 1), and the orientation of each type's within-type expression spread (Layer 2). In human and mouse, the configuration is conserved far beyond chance (observed cross-species disparity 0.52 times the null median, where 1 is chance and lower is more conserved; p < 10⁻⁶). This conservation is carried disproportionately by the genes whose cross-species expression is most conserved, which are enriched for canonical identity transcription factors. Within-type covariance orientation is shared as well: the alignment that best fits the centroids compresses it rather than absorbing it, and correctly matched types agree more than mismatched types in both bases. It replicates on three primate pairs from a single harmonized basal-ganglia atlas (post-rotation similarity below pre-rotation in all three pairs at every dimension tested, each above its null at p < 10⁻⁴); there, under gene-level standardization, the compressed axis moves off housekeeping structure toward cell-identity markers. Per-type divergence rankings behave differently: precise within an atlas but near-uncorrelated across atlases. In a simulation calibrated to the real data, rank recovery peaks at ρ ≈ 0.45 and does not rise as more cells are sampled, so additional cells will not resolve the rankings. Conserved cell-type geometry is measurable and replicates; per-type divergence is not yet resolvable in available atlases, a boundary that constrains how single-atlas divergence estimates should be read.


Keywords: Procrustes analysis, cross-species transcriptomics, cell type evolution, single-cell RNA-seq, cell type divergence, cell type geometry


========================================================================
INTRODUCTION
========================================================================


A cell type occupies a characteristic position in transcriptomic space, set by which genes it expresses and how strongly. We represent each type by a single point, its mean expression across orthologous genes, so the cell types of a species form a configuration of points. The geometry of cell-type identity has two levels. The first is the shape of that configuration: what remains once differences in position, orientation, and overall scale are removed. The second is the orientation of the cloud of cells around each point, which a single-point representation sets aside and which we recover as Layer 2. Throughout, configuration refers to the first level alone. Geometric morphometrics defines exactly this quantity for anatomical forms, where corresponding landmarks on two specimens are superimposed and what survives superimposition is shape[1]. Treating cell types as landmarks and placing both species in a shared space of orthologous genes, Procrustes superimposition aligns the two configurations and returns the conserved shape.

The two levels behave differently. Layer 1 is the arrangement of the cell-type centroids relative to one another. Layer 2 is the orientation of the cloud of cells around each centroid. For each we ask two questions: is the structure conserved between species, and is it recoverable from single-cell atlases as they exist today? The answers differ. The conserved arrangement is reproducible, while the per-type divergence ranking the same framework yields, though widely reported[2,3], is not recoverable from current data.

Comparative single-cell methods answer a different question. SAMap[4], SATURN[5], CAMEX[6], foundation-model[7] and optimal-transport[8] approaches establish which cell type in one species corresponds to which in another, or return a per-type distance between two species' distributions. None fits a single geometric transform across all types at once and reads off what each type contributes to the residual, which is what a geometric-morphometric treatment supplies. That the arrangement itself carries biology is already established within a species: bulk transcriptomes of normal cell types show a tree-like organization that is degraded in cancer[9]. Whether it is conserved across species, and whether that conservation separates into distinct geometric components, is what we test here.

Applying Procrustes superimposition to ontology-matched cell-type centroids, we find the configuration conserved across the primate–rodent divergence and independently within primates; the second layer, the orientation of within-type covariance, conserved as well and replicating on an independent primate atlas; and both carried by the genes whose expression is most conserved across species. The per-type divergence ranking, by contrast, is precise within any single atlas and near-uncorrelated across atlases, and a simulation calibrated to the observed signal bounds what more cells can recover. Conserved cell-type geometry is therefore measurable; which types have diverged most is not, in current data. Concretely, a per-type divergence ranking reported from a single atlas pair should be read as an atlas-specific observation rather than a resolved biological ordering, and this paper marks where that boundary falls.


========================================================================
RESULTS
========================================================================


1. The configuration of cell types is conserved across species

Across 35 cell types matched between human and mouse by Cell Ontology label, the arrangement of cell-type centroids in transcriptomic space is conserved far beyond chance. Representing each type as its mean expression across 16,959 one-to-one orthologs and aligning the two species' configurations by Procrustes superimposition, the observed cross-species disparity is roughly half that of randomized configurations (observed-to-null ratio 0.52; p < 10⁻⁶, one million permutations; Fig 1A,B). Lower ratios indicate stronger conservation. The scale has no absolute interpretation, so 0.52 is read here against the paper's own anchors rather than against a threshold: self-comparison sits near 0, within-species tissue pairs average 0.47, and chance is 1 (S2 Fig). The 1:1 ortholog restriction also means this magnitude characterizes the conserved orthologous portion of the transcriptome, not the transcriptome as a whole.

Fig 1. The cell-type configuration is conserved across two mammalian orders and within primates.
(A) Pipeline. Cell-type centroids are computed independently for human and mouse over 16,959 shared 1:1 orthologs, projected into a 33-component joint PCA space (95.2% variance), aligned by Procrustes superimposition, and read out as per-type residual displacements. (B) The observed cross-species Procrustes distance (61.15) falls far below its 1,000,000-permutation label-shuffle null: obs/null 0.52, where obs/null is the observed distance over the null median, values below 1 indicate conservation, and 1 is chance (p < 10⁻⁶). The human–mouse configuration is conserved well beyond chance. (C) The conservation is not merely lineage-level clustering: permuting labels only within lineage blocks (orange null, versus the grey global null) still leaves the configuration significantly ordered (obs/null 0.67; p < 10⁻⁴). (D) The signal is not specific to the human–mouse pair: a human–mouse-lemur comparison (mouse lemur is a second primate, ~75 Myr) clears the same test (obs/null 0.35; p < 10⁻⁴; n = 15). This panel plots a different species pair, so its observed distance (21.8) is not comparable to the 61.15 in B and C.

Two controls rule out the two measurement explanations most likely to produce this result. The signal is not manufactured by the shared embedding: when each species is embedded independently and the configurations aligned without any joint basis, conservation is at least as strong (S1 Fig). And it is not merely lineage-level clustering (immune cells near immune cells, epithelial near epithelial), because a permutation test that shuffles labels only within lineage blocks, holding coarse structure fixed, still finds the observed configuration significantly ordered (obs/null 0.67; p < 10⁻⁴; Fig 1C). Cell types occupy conserved positions relative to their neighbors, not just conserved neighborhoods. A stricter null that shuffles labels only within marker-similarity groups erodes the signal as those groups are made finer (obs/null 0.52 unrestricted, 0.72 at five groups, 0.90 at ten, and not significant at fifteen, where singleton groups are frozen and contribute identically to observed and null; S5 Fig, S10 Table). A residual therefore persists well beyond lineage membership, but most of the unrestricted signal is attributable to coarse marker structure. Dropping parent labels whose child types are also present, or the children in their place, leaves the configuration conserved at the permutation floor on the reduced landmark sets (obs/null 0.52 at 30 types and 0.54 at 26; each p < 10⁻⁴; S1 Text).

The conservation is not confined to a single atlas pair, and the mammalian reach of the result rests on the human–mouse comparison, which spans the deep primate–rodent divergence (~90 million years[10]). A second pair (human and mouse lemur, a strepsirrhine primate separated from human by roughly 75 million years[10]) clears the same test (obs/null 0.35; p < 10⁻⁴; Fig 1D). It retains the primary's human centroids and so replaces only one arm of the comparison, extending the configuration signal to a different primate lineage rather than replicating it independently. Conserved cell-type configuration is thus a property of more than one species pair: it holds across the primate–rodent divergence (~90 Myr, two mammalian orders), the deepest split tested, and is reproduced independently within primates.

A configuration, though, is only the coarsest description of the geometry. It records where each cell type sits, but not the shape of the variation around it. Whether conservation reaches that finer structure is the next question.


2. Both geometric layers are conserved, and the decomposition replicates on an independent primate atlas

Covariance orientation is conserved as well, though this analysis cannot separate it from position. Beyond where each centroid sits, the configuration of Layer 1, each cell type carries a second geometric feature: the orientation of the cloud of its cells around that centroid, its covariance ellipsoid (Layer 2). Across species these orientations agree above chance, but that agreement could be nothing more than a reflection of the configuration. To test whether it is more, we apply the centroid-optimal rotation to the ellipsoids and re-measure. Were the agreement simply redundant with the centroid alignment, this rotation would absorb it and the re-measured agreement would fall to what random cell-type matching gives. It does not: the rotation compresses the agreement, Krzanowski[11] subspace similarity S, which scores the agreement between the two species' leading k covariance eigenvectors from 0 (orthogonal) to 1 (identical), drops from 0.483 to 0.230 at k = 5, but leaves it well above its null (0.180; p < 10⁻⁴; Fig 2A,B). Relative to their own nulls, however, the agreement is nearly unchanged by the rotation (1.29 before, 1.27 after): the rotation rescales the statistic and its null together rather than isolating a residual. What the comparison establishes is that correctly matched types share covariance orientation more than mismatched types do, in both bases, and that the test after rotation is conditional on the rotation itself rather than a marginal test of Layer 2. The agreement is significant at k = 3 and k = 5 but not at k = 1, where the post-rotation value sits slightly below its own null (0.026 against 0.030; p = 0.77). Excluding ribosomal-protein genes moves the k = 1 point estimate above its null but not to significance (p = 0.065 pre-rotation and 0.089 post-rotation), so the leading unscaled axis in this pair is plausibly ribosomal structure rather than a conserved identity axis; k = 1 supports no conclusion in either direction here, and the gene-level reading of it below is descriptive. A direct test of independence between the two layers is inconclusive at this sample size: across the 35 types the Layer-1 per-type residual and the Layer-2 per-type similarity are uncorrelated (Spearman ρ = −0.27, p = 0.12; S13 Table, T31), which neither establishes independence nor excludes a moderate relationship.

Fig 2. Both geometric layers are conserved, and the two-layer decomposition replicates on an independent primate atlas.
Krzanowski subspace similarity S measures the agreement between two sets of leading covariance eigenvectors (k of them; range 0–1, higher = more aligned); here k ∈ {1, 3, 5}. (A) Per-cell-type heatmap of S (human vs mouse) before the centroid-optimal rotation, at k = 1, 3, 5; warmer cells are types whose covariance orientation agrees more across species. (B) Aggregate S before and after the centroid-optimal rotation, at each k, with each permutation null and p-value. At k = 5 the null falls with the statistic, so the ratio to null is nearly unchanged (1.29 before, 1.27 after). The post-rotation null holds the rotation fixed at its observed value, so this is a conditional test. (C) The decomposition replicates on three same-protocol primate pairs from a harmonized 10x basal-ganglia atlas (Human–Macaque, Human–Marmoset, Macaque–Marmoset), under unscaled and per-gene-standardized weightings: in every pair, at every k, the centroid-optimal rotation compresses the covariance axis while it stays above its permutation null (all p < 10⁻⁴; diamonds mark null means). S14 Table gives the underlying values with the retained dimension, the chance level and the observed-minus-null margin for each cell. Italic fractions (18/55, 7/52, 5/52) give the number of types whose top standardized covariance driver is a canonical identity marker: a minority in every pair. A driver counts as canonical by membership in a fixed brain-marker list applied globally, without checking that the marker is specific to the type it drives, so these index proximity to identity structure rather than validated per-type marker calls; the corresponding unscaled fractions are 0. The list is closed, so a genuine identity marker absent from it goes uncounted and these fractions are lower bounds. The macaque arm also pools two Macaca species at genus level (S1 Text). Under standardization the axis moves off housekeeping structure toward identity markers without resolving to them for most types.

This second layer is estimated from individual cells, which raises a question the configuration does not: is the conserved orientation a property of the biology, or of the particular atlas in which it was measured? We tested it on an independent, same-protocol atlas, the harmonized 10x basal-ganglia atlas of the Human and Mammalian Brain Atlas consortium[12], in three cross-species pairs among human, macaque, and marmoset (52 to 55 matched cell types, each above a 100-cell inclusion threshold; per-type counts run from 108 to 145,491, and in the macaque–marmoset pair from 110 to 83,269). The two-layer structure replicates in all three: the centroid-optimal rotation compresses the covariance agreement at every subspace dimension tested, each beyond its permutation null (all p < 10⁻⁴; Fig 2C). Those p-values test each post-rotation statistic against its own label-shuffle null; the pre-versus-post difference is not itself tested. The macaque arm is a genus-level pool, following the source atlas: six Macaca mulatta donors (382,079 nuclei) together with two Macaca nemestrina donors (166,202 nuclei, 30.3% of macaque cells). Restricting it to the six Macaca mulatta donors and recomputing both affected pairs from the cells reproduces the compression at every subspace dimension under both weightings, with every permutation p at its floor and the compression marginally stronger under restriction (S1 Text). The three pairs come from one atlas and are not independent of one another, but they reproduce the human–mouse finding in a second consortium, protocol, and tissue.

The conserved orientation is not explained by expression magnitude or transcript length. Per-gene standardization removes the dominance of high-mean housekeeping genes, the ribosomal-protein driver that tops the axis in 25 of 35 human–mouse types unscaled drops to 1 of 35, and projecting the transcript-length axis out of the standardized expression leaves the compression essentially unchanged in every pair: post-rotation S shifts by at most 0.06 in absolute terms, and mostly by 0.02 or less, and still clears its null at p < 10⁻⁴, while the residual correlation between the axis and gene length collapses from about 0.24–0.33 to about 0.05–0.11. The length axis is nearly orthogonal to the conserved one. Under standardization the axis moves off housekeeping structure toward cell-identity markers, though a canonical marker is the single strongest driver for only a minority of types, and highest in Human–Macaque (18 of 55). Human–Marmoset and Macaque–Marmoset are both catarrhine–platyrrhine comparisons at the same divergence depth, roughly 43 Myr against roughly 29 Myr for Human–Macaque[10], so these three fractions span two depths rather than three and the difference between 7 of 52 and 5 of 52 sits within a single depth; they are reported descriptively.

Same protocol is the point: a whole-cell to single-nucleus comparison would confound assay with biology, in nuclear data the leading covariance axis tracks transcript length, not identity (S2 Text), so the primate replication, not a cross-protocol pair, is the informative test of Layer 2. In the cross-protocol pair the rotation does not compress the statistic at all: post-rotation S slightly exceeds pre-rotation (S2 Text).

The geometry is conserved at both levels and reproduces on an independent atlas. How widely the configuration itself holds, across the many atlases now available, and across greater evolutionary distances, is the next question.


3. The configuration result is robust across atlases and divergence times

The configuration signal is not specific to one atlas pair; it recurs across independently constructed atlases. Three single-source cross-species replications, a second mouse atlas, a single-nucleus atlas built on an assay independent of both original datasets, and a human harmonized meta-atlas, are each significantly conserved (obs/null 0.45–0.55, each p < 10⁻⁴; Fig 3). Because obs/null is not comparable across analyses with different type counts, each replication is read against the primary restricted to that replication's own matched types rather than against the primary at 35 types: on that basis the three single-source replications sit 0.02 to 0.12 above their matched baselines, while the pooled replication and the two non-replications sit 0.38 to 0.47 above theirs. Replacing one arm of the comparison costs little; replacing both costs more, though the latter group also carries independent technical limitations. Each replication's matched types are also, with one exception, more conserved in the primary than the types it misses, which is why the matched baselines fall below random type sets of the same size (S1 Text). A pooled replication assembled from fifteen collections that share neither original atlas is also significantly conserved, though its heterogeneity-pooled magnitude is weaker and not on the same scale as the single-source pairs (obs/null 0.811, p < 10⁻⁴, n = 22); the signal is present even in data that share nothing with the primary.

Fig 3. The conserved configuration is robust across independent atlases.
Obs/null is the observed cross-species Procrustes distance over its permutation-null median (below 1 indicates conservation; 1, the dashed line, is chance); each bar shows obs/null with n and p. Three single-source replications (a second mouse atlas, Sun 2023; a single-nucleus atlas, PanSci; a human meta-atlas, CellHint) recur near the primary (obs/null 0.45–0.55, each p < 10⁻⁴). A pooled replication drawn from fifteen collections that share neither original atlas (pan-Census) is also significant but weaker and not on the same scale (0.81; p < 10⁻⁴; n = 22). Two datasets do not reach significance (hatched bars) for identifiable technical reasons rather than biology: an underpowered six-type liver atlas (Andrews) and a low-sensitivity microwell atlas (MCA[13] × HCA, a pooled CZ CELLxGENE Census aggregate[14]), the latter localized to the atlas rather than the method by a within-human control.

Two attempts do not reach significance, and in both the cause is technical and identifiable rather than biological. A six-type liver atlas is underpowered by its small landmark count, and at six landmarks the permutation floor is unreachable by construction; a microwell-based atlas has low per-cell sensitivity, and a within-human control localizes that failure to the atlas rather than to the method, since human centroids from two independent atlases still clear their null (obs/null 0.728, p = 0.003) where MCA × HCA does not (S2 Fig). Separately, a human–macaque comparison is consistent in direction but underpowered at twelve matched types and does not clear Bonferroni correction over the 55-member inferential family enumerated in S13 Table (64 numbered tests, less the nine excluded there): raw p = 0.0043 becomes 0.2365 corrected. It is reported in S1 Text and not interpreted further. The primary and the single-source replications survive that correction at their permutation floors.

The configuration, then, recurs across independent atlases, and where it does not, the failure is localizable to how an atlas was built rather than to the biology. This makes the behavior of the per-type readout the same alignment produces, the ranking of which cell types have diverged most, all the more striking, because it shares none of this robustness.


4. Per-type divergence rankings are precise within an atlas but not resolvable across atlases

The same alignment that yields the conserved configuration also yields the single most-requested output of a cross-species comparison, a per-type divergence ranking, ordering cell types by how far each has moved, and it behaves nothing like the global signal. Within one atlas the ranking is strikingly reproducible: every one of the 35 types holds a stable rank, with a median 95% confidence interval of three ranks (Fig 4A; S3 Fig), and no single pre-specified factor we tested, network centrality, sequence conservation, expression-level confounds, and others (S1 Text), explains it, although at 35 types those tests have roughly 37% power against a correlation of 0.30 and so rule out strong rather than moderate associations. Across atlases, that structure does not survive. The ranking is essentially uncorrelated between independent atlas pairs (Spearman ρ = +0.15, +0.19, −0.05, and −0.14; mean +0.04, median +0.05; S1 Text, S1 Table). The four are not mutually independent: each replaces only one arm of the primary comparison. And the types pinned most tightly within an atlas are, if anything, the ones that move most across them (ρ = −0.41, p = 0.073). Precision within a dataset buys nothing across datasets, the signature of a measurement reading out structure specific to the atlas it was made in.

Fig 4. Per-type divergence rankings are precise within an atlas but not resolvable across atlases.
(A) Within-atlas precision: each of the 35 cell types holds a stable divergence rank (median 95% confidence interval = 3 ranks; all 35 stable). (B) Across atlases the ranking does not reproduce: the same types re-order between independent atlas pairs (cross-atlas Spearman ρ: mean +0.04, range −0.14 to +0.19, four pairs). Plotting within-atlas precision (95% CI width) against cross-atlas rank shift, the types pinned most precisely within an atlas tend to move most across them, though at this n the trend is only suggestive (ρ = −0.41; p = 0.073; n = 20). (C) A plant-and-recover simulation identifies the ceiling: at the signal strength of the real data, recovery of the true ranking peaks at ρ ≈ 0.45 and does not rise as more cells are sampled (50–2,000; three signal levels shown). The ceiling bounds what additional cells can buy; it does not on its own establish that signal rather than annotation or composition is the limiting factor.

Reproducible is not the same as accurate, and a plant-and-recover simulation separates the two. Re-sampling the same data recovers the same ranking almost perfectly (test–retest ρ = 0.994), this is the within-atlas precision, reproduced in silico. But when a known divergence ranking is planted and noise added at the signal strength of the real atlases, recovery of the true order peaks at ρ ≈ 0.45 at the signal strength calibrated to the observed data, and does not rise as more cells are sampled, from 50 to 2,000 (Fig 4C). Recovery is not monotone in signal strength, so this is a plateau specific to the calibrated regime rather than a universal ceiling: at the two bracketing signal strengths the deposited grid evaluates, recovery is 0.42 and 0.43 at the same 200 cells per type. More cells sharpen each centroid but cannot recover an ordering the signal does not carry, so sampling depth is not the binding constraint. Its exact value depends on how broadly true divergences are assumed to be spread, but sweeping that assumption over a six-fold range of planted spread, recalibrating the signal at each point, moves the ceiling between 0.45 and 0.50, with the spread used here at the bottom of that range (sweep_spread_results.json, deposited with the code); the qualitative result, that more cells do not help, does not depend on it at all. What the ceiling implies for agreement between two atlases cannot be read off it directly. Multiplying two recovery correlations would assume their errors are independent, and the test–retest value above shows the opposite in the one case these data measure: two estimates of the same truth agree at 0.994, so recovery error is largely shared rather than averaging out. Two atlases do not share a truth, so the cross-atlas case lies between those extremes and nothing deposited here fixes where. What can be said is that the observed agreement is unresolved in either direction: at fifteen to twenty-two matched types the 95% confidence interval on each of the four cross-atlas correlations contains zero and extends past +0.20. At that precision the observed agreement is equally consistent with zero, so the sampling limit is sufficient rather than demonstrated, and the annotation and composition differences documented in S3 Table and S4 Table plausibly account for part of the remainder.

The consequence for how these rankings should be read is concrete. Within a single atlas, with protocol held fixed, the ordering is the expected one: across 100 donor splits the cross-species value exceeded the within-species value in every split (median difference +0.159, 95% CI +0.100 to +0.218; S1 Text). Across two independently constructed human atlases matched to six landmarks the ordering reverses, two atlases of the same species proving less alike (obs/null 0.607) than two different species (0.317; S4 Fig). We report that reversal as an indication that unmatched protocol can dominate the within-species arm, not as a tested result: neither value clears Bonferroni correction over the inferential family (0.484 and 0.193), the difference between them is not itself tested, and both rest on six landmarks, the same count the liver non-replication is called underpowered at. The within-atlas donor split is the valid test of this contrast (S1 Text). A per-type divergence ranking from a single atlas pair is therefore an atlas-specific observation, not a resolved biological ordering. Whether a single true ranking exists and current data cannot resolve it, or per-type divergence is itself context-dependent, a cell type's divergence measured from one tissue mixture need not equal its divergence measured from another, the practical conclusion is identical: resolving which cell types have diverged most will require harmonized, composition-controlled designs across atlases, not deeper sequencing of the ones we have.


5. The conserved configuration is carried by conserved cell-identity genes

Having drawn that boundary, we can say what its measurable side rests on: the conserved configuration is carried by the genes whose expression is most conserved across species. Restricting the configuration to the top conservation quartile preserves the signal. Because the conservation score is estimated on the same centroids that produce the geometry, the selection was tested out of sample: selecting the quartile on one half of the donors and evaluating the configuration on the other half, the selected set outperforms the full unselected gene space in every one of forty split-and-direction combinations and falls below all one hundred expression-matched random sets, retaining a median 90% of the advantage that in-sample selection obtains (S1 Text). The substantive result is independent of that check: these conserved genes are enriched for canonical master and identity transcription factors, above backgrounds matched for both expression level and cell-type specificity (median conservation percentile 0.94, versus 0.54 for an expression-matched and 0.76 for a jointly matched background; Fig 5, S11 Table). The enrichment is a property of this curated set of lineage-defining regulators for these cell types, not of transcription factors as a class: a full human transcription-factor census shows no enrichment against the same expression-matched background, and canonical non-transcription-factor markers of the same types sit at the jointly matched null. Because the set was assembled against the same matched types that define the conservation score, we read this as a quantification of the identity interpretation rather than a discovery about regulation.

C is the cross-species conservation of a gene: the Pearson correlation of its expression profile across the 35 matched cell-type centroids (human vs mouse), computed independently of the Procrustes axes. (A) Distribution of C across 15,940 orthologs, a broad continuum (Hartigan dip[15] D = 0.007, p = 2.8 × 10⁻⁵: a statistically detectable but negligible departure from unimodality at this sample size), with the conserved (top-quartile, Q75 = 0.59) and divergent (Q25 = 0.10) sets marked. Restricting the configuration analysis to the top-C quartile preserves the conserved signal (main text), so the conserved arrangement is carried by these identity genes.

Taken together, the results draw a clean line through what current data can and cannot support. The configuration of cell types is conserved across two mammalian orders, the primate–rodent divergence of roughly 90 million years, replicates at the level of within-type covariance across primate atlases, and is carried by conserved identity genes. The per-type divergence ranking that the same framework produces, and that is widely reported, is precise within an atlas but not recoverable across atlases, and more cells do not resolve it. Conserved cell-type geometry is measurable; the finer question of which types have diverged most is, for now, beyond the reach of the atlases we have.


========================================================================
DISCUSSION
========================================================================


Treating cell types as landmarks and comparing species by superimposition separates two things that a single similarity score conflates: whether the arrangement of cell types is conserved, and whether the shape of variation around each type is conserved. Both are: the alignment that best matches the centroids compresses the covariance orientation rather than absorbing it, and correctly matched types agree more than mismatched types do in both bases. Whether the two layers carry independent conserved information is left open, because the test after rotation is conditional on that rotation. That the covariance result reproduces across three primate pairs from a single harmonized atlas, and that under gene-level standardization the conserved axis moves off housekeeping structure toward cell-identity markers, indicates a shared biological organization rather than a statistical coincidence of one dataset.

The more consequential result is a boundary. The per-type divergence ranking, which cell types have diverged most between species, is among the most natural quantities to extract from a cross-species comparison, and it is widely reported. We find it precise within any single atlas but not reproducible across atlases, and a simulation shows why: at the signal strength of real data, the recoverable correlation between a true divergence ranking and the estimated one peaks at ρ ≈ 0.45 and does not rise as more cells are sampled. The instability is a property of the information available in current atlases, not of sampling. In practical terms, between-atlas variation is large enough to erase the per-type ordering: the four cross-atlas rank correlations have confidence intervals that contain zero and extend past +0.20, so a divergence ranking from a single atlas pair should be read as atlas-specific rather than as a resolved biological ordering. This does not mean cross-species divergence is unmeasurable in principle; it means that measuring it will require harmonized, multi-atlas designs that control annotation and composition across sources. It is a design constraint, not a deeper sequencer.

Three bounds delimit these conclusions. First, the covariance-layer replication is demonstrated across primates; whether it extends to greater divergence, such as the primate–rodent split at which the configuration is conserved, remains to be tested with same-protocol atlases at that distance. Second, the divergence-ranking result is a statement about currently available atlases, established on the species and tissues examined here; it identifies a resolution limit under present data rather than a permanent one. Third, the framework compares configurations of ontology-matched types and inherits the granularity of those annotations: a coarsely annotated type and a finely annotated one are not equivalent landmarks[16], which is one reason harmonized annotation is the operative requirement for resolving per-type divergence. Each of these is a boundary on scope, and none is contradicted by the results within it.

The conserved configuration is carried disproportionately by the most cross-species-conserved genes, which are enriched for master and identity transcription factors. We read this as a quantification of the identity interpretation, not as a discovery about regulation: the conserved arrangement is the arrangement of cell identities, encoded by conserved regulatory genes. The enriched genes are, in part, conserved by the same evolutionary pressures that conserve the geometry, and we do not separate those. The value of the result is that it ties an abstract geometric signal to a concrete and independently sensible molecular basis.

The two-layer decomposition is a general instrument: any pair of cell-type collections in a shared feature space can be compared this way, and the same machinery that here separates conserved configuration from conserved dispersion could compare configurations across conditions, developmental stages, or perturbations. The immediate methodological contribution, though, is the pair of findings taken together: that conserved cell-type geometry is real, replicable, and identity-bearing, and that the per-type divergence structure the same method produces is not yet resolvable in available data. Reporting both, rather than the positive alone, is what the measurement supports; it marks the line between what current single-cell atlases can establish about cell-type evolution and what they cannot yet.

Materials and Methods

Ethics statement

This study is a computational reanalysis of previously published, publicly available single-cell and single-nucleus RNA-sequencing datasets; no new human or animal data were generated. All datasets were obtained in de-identified form from public repositories, where they were deposited by the original studies under their own institutional ethical and animal-care approvals. Because only existing, publicly available, de-identified data were analyzed, no additional ethics approval was required.

The framework used throughout is landmark-based Procrustes superimposition of ontology-matched cell-type centroids, with a two-layer decomposition into configuration and within-type covariance; we refer to it as CellWarp.

Data and cell-type matching

Primary human data were from Tabula Sapiens[17] (v1.0; 483,152 cells, 24 donors, 10x Chromium and Smart-seq2) and primary mouse data from Tabula Muris Senis[18] (whole-organism, multiple ages, 10x Chromium and Smart-seq2), both accessed via CZ CELLxGENE Census[14] (version 2025-11-08), which is the reproducibility anchor for these datasets. The mouse-lemur extension used Tabula Microcebus[19] (Microcebus murinus, 10x 3′ v2, accessed via CZ CELLxGENE Discover), paired with the primary Tabula Sapiens human centroids restricted to the fifteen types matched between the two atlases. The ortholog set came from BioMart (16,655 human and lemur pairs, 13,796 retained after filtering); centroids used the same 500-cell inclusion threshold and 2,000-cell cap as the primary, with a minimum realized count of 546 cells; the joint PCA over the thirty stacked centroids retained fifteen components at the 95% cumulative-variance threshold, and significance was assessed against 10,000 label permutations. Because it retains the primary's human arm, it replaces only one arm of the comparison. The primate covariance replication (Layer 2) used the harmonized 10x basal-ganglia atlas of the Human and Mammalian Brain Atlas consortium (human, macaque, and marmoset). Because both atlases integrate across multiple tissues per organism, each cell-type centroid aggregates across tissue contexts rather than reflecting a single organ; because Tabula Muris Senis spans several ages whereas the human atlas is predominantly adult, an age-composition difference between the two species is not controlled. Like tissue-composition and annotation-granularity differences, this falls on the per-type residuals rather than the global configuration, and is one more reason those residuals are read here as atlas-specific rather than as resolved biological divergence[20].

Cross-species correspondence was defined operationally by shared Cell Ontology[21] label: a type was included when it appeared under an identical canonical Cell Ontology display string in both atlases and reached at least 500 cells per species after quality filtering. No marker re-annotation was applied. This treats a shared label as a correspondence, not an assertion of evolutionary homology[22]; for broad parent labels (for example stromal cell, epithelial cell, generic monocyte) it may aggregate subtypes whose cross-species correspondence is not established, so residuals at those labels confound divergence with cross-species subtype-composition differences that the framework does not separate. Cells were restricted to those flagged as primary data in the Census, which removes 66.7% of both collections by excluding cells duplicated across overlapping datasets, and to a normal disease state, which removes none in these two atlases. Under those filters, 66 labels were shared across the two atlases and 35 met the 500-cell threshold, spanning immune/hematopoietic, epithelial, stromal/mesenchymal, endothelial, and metabolic/parenchymal lineages (full list, S5 Table). A 6-type subset (B cell, CD4+ T cell, CD8+ T cell, endothelial cell, hepatocyte, macrophage) is used where a smaller matched n is required.

Human–mouse one-to-one orthologs were obtained from Ensembl BioMart[23] release 115 (GRCh38.p14 / GRCm39; accessed 2026-03-12); only strict 1:1 relationships were retained (paralog averaging was not performed, which deflates rather than inflates any conservation signal). Restricting to this ortholog set is a scope condition rather than a controlled variable: because the permutation null is computed on the same gene space, any influence the restriction has raises observed and null agreement together and divides out, so this design can neither create nor detect it. The result is conservation within the 1:1 ortholog space, which excludes lineage-specific expansions and species-specific families. After intersecting with genes present in both atlases, the operating gene space comprised 16,959 orthologs. The full ortholog table is archived as CSV and is the reproducibility anchor for all gene-space definitions. A maximum of 2,000 cells per type per species was randomly subsampled (seed 42) before centroid computation for the primary human–mouse analysis; the same 2,000-cell cap therefore also bounds the cells entering the primary within-type covariances, since it is applied when the atlas is retrieved rather than at centroid computation. The primate replication is not capped, so the primary and the replication are computed under different per-type sampling regimes.

The Procrustes framework

Cell-type centroids were computed as the mean of log1p-transformed, library-size-normalized (counts per 10,000; CP10K) expression over all cells of a type within a species, independently per species with no cross-species alignment, integration, or batch correction; the cross-species signal is therefore present in the raw centroid displacement, not introduced by alignment. Because centroids are means of log1p expression, high-mean ubiquitous genes (ribosomal, housekeeping) contribute disproportionately to centroid and within-type-covariance geometry, which is why the leading unscaled within-type axis is ribosomal-dominated (below). The configuration itself is not an artifact of this high-mean structure: excluding ribosomal-protein genes, or the broader set of housekeeping genes, leaves it as strong or stronger (obs/null 0.501 and 0.479, versus 0.522; each p < 10⁻⁴; S7 Table), and per-gene standardization that equalizes all gene variances, the most stringent removal of high-mean dominance, leaves it conserved (0.606 under Scheme A and 0.487 under Scheme B, the two standardizations defined in Materials and Methods; S9 Table, S1 Text). High-mean genes do not manufacture the signal; if anything, removing them sharpens it. The configuration is likewise robust to how cells are aggregated into centroids: recomputing the centroids as pseudo-bulk profiles (aggregating counts per type before normalization and log transformation, rather than averaging per-cell log values) leaves the configuration clearly conserved under both an equal-cell-weighted and a library-size-weighted aggregation (obs/null 0.49 and 0.62; each p < 10⁻⁴; S1 Text).

Prior to superimposition, principal component analysis was applied jointly to the stacked matrix of 70 centroids (35 types × 2 species) in the 16,959-gene space, retaining the number of components reaching 95% cumulative variance (33 components, 95.2%). Dimensionality reduction is required for numerical stability: with only 70 landmark points in thousands of dimensions, Procrustes alignment is otherwise underdetermined. Rankings were stable across k = 10–50 (S2 Fig). Procrustes superimposition was implemented directly (not via a library routine) to expose per-type residuals in the original gene space. Each configuration was centered; the cross-covariance M = XcᵀYc of the centered human (Xc) and mouse (Yc) configurations was decomposed by singular value decomposition M = UΣVᵀ; the rotation R = VDUᵀ (D diagonal with final entry sign(det(VUᵀ)), others 1) algebraically guarantees det(R) = +1, precluding reflections; and the rotated mouse configuration was scaled by the optimal full-Procrustes scalar. Procrustes distance is the square root of the summed squared residual displacements, so it and the per-type residuals, the Euclidean distances between aligned human–mouse centroid pairs, are on the same scale (higher = more divergent); the observed-to-null ratio is a ratio of two such distances. The custom implementation was verified against scipy.spatial.procrustes to machine precision.

Significance was assessed by a permutation null that shuffles cell-type labels (not cells within types) in the mouse configuration, testing whether the global transform aligns all types simultaneously better than chance; the empirical p-value uses the conservative (n≤ + 1)/(nperm + 1) estimator[24]. The primary human–mouse test used 1,000,000 permutations (observed distance 61.15; nearest null 98.88; zero permutations ≤ observed; p < 10⁻⁶); replications, extensions, and secondary tests used 10,000 permutations (floor p < 10⁻⁴). The observed-to-null ratio (observed distance / median permuted distance) is the effect size; because the null median varies with type count, obs/null is not comparable across analyses with different n. A lineage-stratified null (labels shuffled only within five lineage blocks) tests positional correspondence beyond lineage membership (obs/null 0.668, p < 10⁻⁴). Robustness was confirmed by bootstrap resampling (50% of cells per type; Procrustes-distance CV = 0.004 over 100 iterations, which bounds the observed distance and not the observed-to-null ratio, since the permutation null was not recomputed per iteration) and by leave-one-out cross-validation with PCA and rotation refit per fold (35/35 held-out types predicted better than random pairing). An independent-PCA variant (per-species PCA aligned via loadings, label-free) and a Mantel test on pairwise-distance matrices are reported as artifact-ruling controls (S1 Fig; S1 Text). Ten mechanistic hypotheses for the per-type residual were tested using DoRothEA [25], MSigDB [26], STRING v12 [27], the UCSC phastCons 20-way alignment [28], and the ChEMBL [29] and DrugBank [30] target sets; the citations are to the resources rather than to the releases used, which were not recorded for four of them (see Data and code availability). Where a hypothesis comprised multiple sub-tests these were corrected by the Benjamini–Hochberg false-discovery-rate procedure [31]. None reached significance; the full set is reported in S1 Text.

The two-layer decomposition

Layer 1 is centroid position (the primary Procrustes result). For Layer 2, single cells of each type were projected into the same 33-component joint PCA space, the per-type 33×33 covariance was computed (all cells; eigendecomposition by NumPy linalg.eigh), and the leading k eigenvectors define each type's covariance-ellipsoid orientation. Cross-species alignment was quantified by a k-normalized form of the Krzanowski[11] subspace-similarity statistic, S(k) = ‖AᵀB‖F² / k (A, B the k leading eigenvectors for the two species; range 0–1, higher = better alignment) at k ∈ {1, 3, 5}, against a 10,000-permutation label-shuffle null. S(k) is computed for each matched type and aggregated across types as an unweighted mean, and the primate replication uses the same rule. Unconstrained by the centroid alignment, Layer 2 was significant at k = 5 (S = 0.483 vs null 0.375; p < 10⁻⁴) and k = 3, but not k = 1, where the observed value sits at 0.129 against a null of 0.119 before rotation and 0.026 against 0.030 after it, the post-rotation point estimate falling just below its null (p = 0.77). The centroid-optimal rotation R was then applied to each type's mouse-side covariance eigenvectors and compared to the unrotated human eigenvectors: this compresses aggregate alignment from S = 0.483 to S = 0.230 at k = 5, and the compressed statistic remains above its null (0.180; p < 10⁻⁴). For k-normalized S with k = 5 in a 33-dimensional space, the expected value for independent random subspaces is k/p = 0.152, so the label-shuffle null sits modestly above chance and the observed value modestly above the null. This post-rotation null holds R fixed at its observed value and shuffles cell-type labels, testing whether the residual agreement exceeds what matching random types under the same rotation gives; the test is therefore conditional on R, not a marginal test of Layer 2.

The leading conserved axis per type (CPC1; the top eigenvector of the cell-count-weighted sum of within-type covariances, that is, a pooled-covariance leading principal component and not Flury's common principal component) was projected back to gene space and ranked by loading (S6 Table). Under unscaled weighting 25 of 35 types have a ribosomal-protein rank-1 driver, a property of the unscaled covariance, not a housekeeping claim, which per-gene standardization under Scheme B removes (1 of 35 ribosomal); excluding ribosomal-protein genes leaves the compression significant (post-rotation S = 0.234 vs null 0.180; p < 10⁻⁴). Under standardization the axis moves off housekeeping structure, but a canonical identity marker is the single strongest driver for only a minority of types (primate fractions, Fig 2C). The conserved covariance orientation is thus a real property of these data, though not one this design separates from centroid position; nor do we claim a single universal driver set for it, because the specific drivers depend on the molecular pool a protocol samples (whole-cell versus nuclear; S2 Text).

Primate replication (basal ganglia)

The Layer 2 decomposition was replicated on the harmonized 10x basal-ganglia atlas of the Human and Mammalian Brain Atlas consortium across three same-protocol cross-species pairs (Human–Macaque, Human–Marmoset, Macaque–Marmoset; 52 to 55 matched cell types, a few hundred to over 100,000 cells per type). The three pairs are drawn from this one atlas and are not mutually independent, but the atlas is independent of the human–mouse primary in consortium, protocol, and tissue. For each pair, centroids and per-type covariances were computed on the shared ortholog space, joint PCA was fit per pair at the 95% variance threshold, and the two-layer statistics S(k) were computed pre- and post-centroid-rotation at k ∈ {1, 3, 5} against a 10,000-permutation label-shuffle null, as in the primary analysis, under two weightings, unscaled and per-gene standardized. In every pair, at every k, the centroid-optimal alignment compressed the covariance axis while remaining above its null (all p < 10⁻⁴). Under unscaled weighting the rank-1 CPC1 driver was never a canonical identity marker; under standardization a canonical marker becomes the single strongest driver for a minority of types, 18 of 55 (Human–Macaque), 7 of 52 (Human–Marmoset), and 5 of 52 (Macaque–Marmoset), so the axis moves off housekeeping structure toward identity without resolving to markers for most types. The macaque arm of this atlas pools two Macaca species at genus level; restricting it to the six Macaca mulatta donors and recomputing from the cells reproduces the compression at every k under both weightings with every p at its floor, and raises these fractions to 25 of 54 and 8 of 51, the retained subspace dimension falling by one in each pair and one sparsely sampled type dropping below the inclusion threshold. The restricted run's producers and outputs are deposited with the basal-ganglia record (S1 Text). A driver counts as a canonical identity marker by membership in a fixed brain-marker list, applied without checking that the marker is specific to the type it drives; the fractions are therefore a coarse index of proximity to identity structure rather than a validated per-type marker rate (S1 Text). Cross-species type correspondence in this atlas is inherited from the consortium's own consensus taxonomy, which was constructed by cross-species integration, so the safeguard that centroids are computed with no cross-species alignment holds for the centroids here but not for the matching between them; integration optimizes centroid correspondence rather than within-type covariance orientation, which is the quantity Layer 2 measures. Restricting each pair to the types whose labels an independent cell-ontology lookup corroborates, which discards more than half of them, leaves the covariance agreement above a permutation null built on the restricted subset at every subspace dimension in every pair (S1 Text).

To test directly whether the conserved axis is a shared transcript-length or length-biased-capture artifact rather than biology, the gene-length direction was projected out of each cell's per-gene-standardized expression before every within-type covariance was computed, and the full two-layer pipeline, joint-PCA basis, centroid-optimal rotation, k ∈ {1, 3, 5}, and the 10,000-permutation null re-run on the residualized data, was repeated unchanged. The length direction was the unit-norm gene-length vector in the shared ortholog space, parameterized as centered-log10 length (which carries the monotone length gradient; a raw-base-pair variant, outlier-dominated, is a robustness reference), for canonical transcript length, genomic span, and both jointly. Across all three primate pairs and the primary human–mouse pair, and all three length variants, the post-rotation compression is essentially unchanged: post-rotation S shifts by at most 0.06 (mostly ≤ 0.02) and continues to clear its null at p < 10⁻⁴ wherever the un-residualized statistic did, while the residual correlation between the axis loading and gene length collapses from about 0.24–0.33 to about 0.05–0.11, confirming the direction was removed. The conserved axis is therefore nearly orthogonal to the length axis. Both parameterizations reach the same verdict, so the conclusion does not depend on the length parameterization (L2_LENGTH_RESIDUALIZATION.md, deposited with the basal-ganglia analysis).

Each type's covariance is estimated from that type's own cells, so the aggregate is weighted toward well-sampled types; the great majority are well sampled (42/55, 37/52, 39/52 types have ≥ 500 cells in both species), and restricting the aggregate to those types leaves the compression and its significance unchanged in all three pairs and both weightings (post-rotation still below pre-rotation at k = 5, all p < 10⁻⁴; layer2_lowN_robustness.json). Per-pair statistics and CPC1 driver classifications are deposited with the basal-ganglia analysis (Data and code availability).

Simulation

Pipeline statistical properties were characterized by a plant-and-recover simulation. Synthetic paired configurations were built by placing n cell-type centroids in a 50-factor latent space and embedding them into a 500-gene space via a scaled Gaussian projection; a Haar-distributed orthogonal rotation simulated the cross-species transform; and per-type Gaussian noise, log-normally distributed across types with a log-scale parameter of 1.0 (a 65-fold ratio between the largest and smallest planted divergence, or 20.6-fold between the 5th and 95th percentiles), planted a known per-type divergence ranking. A single signal-strength parameter controls the noise level; it was calibrated by grid search with interpolation to reproduce the observed primary obs/null of 0.522 (calibrated signal ≈ 3.68). Global detection power was 100% at the calibrated signal across n = 15, 25, 35, with a well-calibrated null (false-positive rate 4.8% at α = 0.05). Re-sampling the same synthetic centroids gave a within-atlas test–retest of ρ = 0.994, the in-silico analog of the high within-atlas precision seen in the real data. Recovery of the planted ranking, by contrast, Spearman ρ between planted and recovered orders, reached ρ ≈ 0.45 at the calibrated signal and did not rise with cells sampled per type (50–2,000; the small decline across that range is within the spread across replicates at the calibrated signal); recovery is not monotone in the signal parameter, and the deposited grid does not evaluate at the calibrated signal: it gives ρ ≈ 0.42 at signal 3.0 and ρ ≈ 0.43 at signal 5.0 at the same 200 cells per type, so the two bracketing values understate the calibrated one that lies between them. The ceiling is a plateau specific to this signal-to-spread regime, set by the recovery problem rather than by cell sampling. Because the planted per-type spread is an assumption rather than a measured quantity, the exact ceiling is model-dependent; sweeping it over a six-fold range and recalibrating the signal at each point gives ceilings between 0.45 and 0.50, with the spread used here at the bottom of that range (sweep_spread_results.json, deposited with the code), and the qualitative result, that additional cells do not raise it, holds throughout. The ceiling is also a property of a model calibrated to reproduce one summary statistic, and it presupposes that a single true ranking exists, which the plant-and-recover design requires. The recovery-ceiling and detection-power panels are Fig 4C and S1 Fig; per-condition summaries in S2 Table.

Statistical analysis

The significance threshold is 0.05 except where a stricter level is stated. Coherence and replication tests are label-permutation tests against the null distribution of the Procrustes statistic; where the observed value falls outside every permutation drawn, the p-value is reported as a bound at the permutation floor rather than as an exact value. Correlations are Spearman rank correlations unless otherwise stated.

Sixty-four tests were performed in total. Fifty-five of them constitute the principal inferential family and carry Bonferroni-corrected p-values taken over that family; the nine excluded are those that do not yield a single p-value for a single hypothesis: five report a pass rate over repeated sub-tests, and four are descriptive or simulation-diagnostic quantities that make no inferential claim. Four in-family rows are exceptions in the other direction and are named in S13 Table. The full inventory, with each test's raw and corrected p-value and its family membership, is given in S13 Table. Two analyses in S1 Text instead apply false-discovery-rate correction within their own sets of sub-tests, and are identified as such there [31].

Dated internal analysis plans were committed to the repository before the corresponding statistics were computed. Separately, "pre-specified" throughout this paper denotes a design or threshold decision fixed before the corresponding result was observed; the deposited plans document a subset of these. No analysis was registered with an external public pre-registration registry; some of the deposited plans cover analyses reported elsewhere or held in reserve rather than in this paper, and the full classification of every script, analysis, and plan (paper, banked, other-project, exploratory) is provided in the repository as SCOPE.md.

Analysis code was run under Python 3.12.12 with a global random seed of 42, using numpy 2.4.3, scipy 1.17.1, scikit-learn 1.8.0, statsmodels 0.14.6, scanpy 1.12, anndata 0.12.10 and pandas 2.3.3; package versions are given in S12 Table, and the pinned dependency manifests are deposited with the code. Code and archived outputs are identified under Data and code availability.

Conserved-contribution and identity-gene analysis

To ask whether the conserved configuration is carried by particular genes, each gene was assigned a cross-species conservation score C: the Pearson correlation, across the 35 matched cell-type centroids, between its human and mouse expression profiles. Because Pearson correlation is mean-centered and scale-invariant, C captures the pattern of a gene's cell-type specificity rather than its overall level, and it is computed directly on the centroids, independently of the joint-PCA and Procrustes axes, so it does not presuppose the geometric result it is used to explain. Genes with a constant profile in either species (no defined correlation) were excluded, leaving 15,940 of 16,959 orthologs. No detection-breadth filter is applied, so a gene detected in few centroids can reach a large |C| on little evidence; a sensitivity analysis restricting to genes detected in every matched type in both species is reported in S1 Text, and the master-regulator enrichment holds under it on a smaller set of factors. The conservation score, the quartile thresholds, and the positive-control gene list described below were all fixed in a dated analysis plan before any of these checks were computed; that plan and its repository timestamp are the pre-specification of record.

Genes in the top quartile of C were taken as the conserved set and those in the bottom quartile as the divergent set (3,985 genes each). Re-running the configuration analysis on each set tests whether conservation, rather than divergence, carries the geometric signal: the conserved set is far more coherent than the divergent set (observed-to-null 0.38 versus 0.71), and more coherent than expression-matched random gene sets of the same size (0.53). This contrast is partly expected, since genes are selected on cross-species correlation, which is related to the quantity being measured; it is therefore reported as a contrast (conserved versus divergent, conserved versus matched-random) rather than as an absolute effect, and a derangement null that breaks the gene-to-geometry correspondence while preserving both marginal distributions confirms the association is not an artifact of the selection (S1 Text).

The substantive claim is independent of that geometric re-run: the most-conserved genes are enriched for the transcription factors that define cell identity. This was tested with a pre-specified positive control of 73 canonical lineage-defining and master transcription factors, fixed in the analysis plan before testing, by comparing their median conservation percentile against a null of gene sets matched to the same genes on mean expression (an equal-frequency 20-bin match). The transcription factors sit far above the matched null (median conservation percentile 0.94 versus 0.54), exceeding it in every one of one million matched draws (p < 10⁻⁶). A post-hoc robustness check, matching the null jointly on expression and cell-type specificity (a 10-by-10-bin match, not part of the pre-specified plan), gives the same conclusion against a higher bar (null median 0.76). The enrichment is therefore not explained by the transcription factors being merely highly expressed or merely cell-type-specific. It is, however, specific to this curated set: a full human transcription-factor census of 1,217 genes in the ortholog space shows no enrichment against the expression-matched background (median percentile 0.53), and canonical non-transcription-factor markers sit at the jointly matched null. The 73 were assembled against the same 35 matched types that define the conservation score, and that dependence is not separable by these nulls.

Two checks establish that C itself is a stable quantity rather than an artifact of one dataset's donors. Splitting each species' donors into two disjoint halves (each donor kept whole, 100 random splits) and recomputing C on each half, the per-gene scores agree across halves (median cross-half Spearman 0.80), and the transcription-factor enrichment recovers in all 200 half-datasets. The distribution of C across genes is a broad continuum rather than a conserved/divergent dichotomy (Hartigan dip D = 0.007). C is sensitive to assay, as expected for a correlation over expression profiles: estimates from 10x-only and Smart-seq2-only data agree only moderately (Spearman 0.59). The primary analysis does not hold assay fixed. Of the 62,708 human cells entering the primary centroids, 93.0% are 10x and 7.0% are Smart-seq2 or Smart-seq3; of the 59,745 mouse cells, 33.0% are Smart-seq2. All are whole-cell protocols, so the whole-cell versus single-nucleus distinction drawn in S2 Text is unaffected, but a within-assay component is present in both species and is not controlled.

S3 Table. CellHint rank reversal analysis identifies annotation ontology and tissue composition as systematic drivers of cross-atlas per-type divergence ranking differences.
Per-cell-type comparison of primary (Tabula Sapiens / Tabula Muris Senis) and CellHint per-type divergence rankings for 15 matched cell types, including rank reversal magnitude, tissue counts, and annotation mapping details. Three factors explain the rank reversals: (1) annotation ontology mismatch: CellHint collapses multiple subtypes into broader categories (e.g., 10+ epithelial subtypes into a single "epithelial cell" with 9,537 cells; fibroblast at 88% cardiac tissue versus the primary analysis's separate "fibroblast of cardiac tissue"); (2) tissue composition bias: CellHint neutrophils are liver-only (tissue-resident phenotype) versus multi-tissue in the primary analysis, and CellHint plasma cells are 89% intestinal (IgA-secreting); (3) tissue count correlation with rank reversal magnitude (Spearman ρ = −0.526, p = 0.044), where types drawn from fewer tissues show the largest reversals. The global CellHint Procrustes signal is atlas-robust (obs/null = 0.448, p < 10⁻⁴, n = 15), but per-type residual ranking is sensitive to centroid definition, an expected property of any centroid-based metric. Confidence categorization: LOW = |rank diff| ≥ 9 AND tissue/ontology mismatch identified; MODERATE = |rank diff| 5–8 with direct annotation mapping; HIGH = |rank diff| ≤ 4 with direct annotation mapping. CellHint Tissue Count column: tissue counts for all 15 rows (provided in the accompanying repository); these feed into the Spearman ρ = −0.526 calculation. Primary Rank columns: first column is the 35-type primary rank projected to the 15-type subset (used for Rank Difference, characterizing the as-originally-published disagreement magnitude); second is a fresh 15-type Procrustes re-run for matched-baseline comparison.

S4 Table. Progressive harmonization of CellHint ranking correlation.
Four harmonization levels were applied to the CellHint per-type ranking comparison. Level 0: both primary and CellHint Procrustes analyses were recomputed on the shared 15-type set, ensuring residuals derive from the same-dimensional PCA space (ρ = −0.139, p = 0.621, n = 15). Level 1: "T cell" excluded due to heterogeneous tissue composition across atlases (ρ = −0.037, p = 0.899, n = 14). Level 2: remaining types restricted to shared tissues (ρ = −0.042, p = 0.897, n = 12). Level 3: cell counts capped at minimum across atlases (no change; primary counts already below CellHint counts after tissue restriction; seeds 1–4 confirmed zero variance). The original ρ = −0.386 (reported in S3 Table) was inflated by comparing residuals from incompatible PCA spaces (35-type vs 15-type). After harmonization, the correlation is near zero, indicating noisy but not systematically inverted per-type rankings.

Substantiates the Results section 2 statement that comparing whole-cell to single-nucleus data confounds biology with assay: in nuclear data the leading within-type covariance axis tracks transcript length rather than cell identity, which is why the same-protocol primate replication rather than the protocol switch is the informative test of the covariance layer.
