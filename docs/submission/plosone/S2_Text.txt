S2 Text. Cross-protocol length bias: why the whole-cell versus single-nucleus comparison is uninformative for the covariance layer.

This note substantiates one sentence in Results §2 — that comparing whole-cell to single-nucleus data confounds biology with assay, and that in nuclear data the leading within-type covariance axis is dominated by transcript length rather than cell identity. It is a scoping argument for why the same-protocol primate replication (Fig 2C), not the protocol-switch comparison, is the informative test of Layer 2. It does not relitigate the covariance result.

Setup. The primary human atlas (Tabula Sapiens) is 10x Chromium whole-cell data; the PanSci atlas is EasySci single-nucleus RNA-seq. Applying the two-layer decomposition across this pair is therefore a protocol switch (whole-cell versus single-nucleus), not an independent same-protocol pair. Nuclear and cytoplasmic RNA sample different molecular pools: single-nucleus libraries are enriched for nascent, intron-retaining transcripts and deplete the mature, highly expressed cytoplasmic transcripts (ribosomal-protein mRNAs prominent among them) that dominate whole-cell covariance.

The Krzanowski statistic clears its null but the axis is not shared. On the PanSci pair the Layer-2 covariance-orientation statistic is significant both before and after the centroid-optimal rotation (pre-rotation k = 5: S = 0.396 versus null mean 0.302, p < 10⁻⁴; post-rotation k = 5: S = 0.402 versus null mean 0.360, p < 10⁻⁴). This is a cross-protocol sensitivity check rather than an independent replication, because the PanSci Layer-2 analysis is computed against the same Tabula Sapiens human-10x covariance used in the primary analysis. Critically, the leading within-type driver genes differ across the protocol switch: 25 of 35 cell types are ribosomal-dominated on the rank-1 CPC1 axis in whole-cell 10x, versus 0 of 16 in PanSci single-nucleus. The nuclear rank-1 drivers are instead long, intron-rich lineage genes (for example ARHGAP15, DOCK2, MBNL1, DPYD, KCNIP4, CALCRL, SPTBN1). The statistic being significant while the driver genes are protocol-dependent is exactly the pattern of a conserved covariance *statistic* whose dominant *biological axis* is not shared across the assay.

Length disparity in the nuclear drivers. The nuclear rank-1 CPC1 drivers are far longer than the short ribosomal-protein genes that dominate the whole-cell axis. On the tracked gene-length table, the PanSci rank-1 driver set has a median genomic span of 334,401 bp versus 11,898 bp for the ribosomal-protein genes (a ~28-fold disparity), and a median canonical transcript length of 4,392 bp versus 994 bp (~4.4-fold). Because single-nucleus capture over-represents long, intron-retaining transcripts, the leading covariance axis in nuclear data tracks transcript/gene length rather than a cell-identity program. Consistent with a length component in the covariance axis even within a single protocol, in the same-protocol primate atlas the |CPC1| gene loading correlates with gene length (pooled Spearman ρ ≈ 0.34 for canonical transcript length and ≈ 0.44 for genomic span, per-type p < 0.05 in every type), and this correlation attenuates under per-gene standardization (ρ ≈ 0.24), the same standardization that resolves the axis to identity markers (Results §2).

[Transparency note: an internal length-bias analysis (CC-1/CC-1b) reported a "44×" length disparity. That exact figure was not reproduced from the tracked artifacts available here; on the rank-1 CPC1 driver set the tracked gene-length table gives 28× (genomic span) and 4.4× (canonical transcript), the same direction and order of magnitude. The main-text sentence does not depend on the precise multiplier; the qualitative claim — nuclear covariance axis dominated by transcript length — holds on the tracked numbers. The exact multiplier should be reconciled against the CC-1b source before any precise figure is stated.]

Variance-explained asymmetry. The fraction of within-type variance carried by CPC1 differs sharply between the two sides of the protocol switch (for example, for B cell the CPC1 variance fraction is 0.083 in Tabula Sapiens 10x versus 0.372 in PanSci single-nucleus, a ~4.5-fold asymmetry; asymmetries up to ~8× occur in the whole-cell analysis, e.g. stromal cell). A shared axis direction with very different variance fractions means the two assays devote different amounts of within-type variance to the nominally "aligned" axis.

Incommensurable basis. Taken together — a length-driven axis in nuclear data, a ribosomal/identity-driven axis in whole-cell data, and a large variance-explained asymmetry — the two covariance bases are not commensurable across the protocol switch. The Krzanowski subspace statistic can clear its permutation null (subspaces overlap enough to beat a label shuffle) while the underlying biological axis is not the same axis in both assays. This is why the whole-cell-versus-single-nucleus comparison cannot establish a shared, conserved, identity-bearing covariance axis, and why the same-protocol primate replication (three all-10x basal-ganglia pairs, Fig 2C) is the informative test. There, holding the protocol fixed removes the length/assay confound, and the conserved axis resolves to cell-identity markers under standardization.
