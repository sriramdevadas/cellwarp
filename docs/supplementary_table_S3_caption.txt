Supplementary Table S3. CellHint¹⁶ rank reversal analysis identifies annotation ontology and tissue composition as systematic drivers of cross-atlas rigidity ranking differences, related to Figure 3.
Per-cell-type comparison of primary (Tabula Sapiens¹⁸ / Tabula Muris Senis²⁰) and CellHint¹⁶ rigidity rankings for 15 matched cell types, including rank reversal magnitude, tissue counts, and annotation mapping details. Two primary-rank columns are reported: "Primary Rank (of 15)" — derived from the 35-type primary Procrustes residuals re-ranked among the 15 shared types (the naïve comparison, Spearman ρ = −0.386); and "Primary Rank (matched 15-type Procrustes)" — derived from a Procrustes re-run on the same 15-type set, so that primary and CellHint residuals live in the same-dimensional PCA subspace (matched baseline, Spearman ρ = −0.139; see also Table S4). Three factors explain the rank reversals: (1) annotation ontology mismatch — CellHint¹⁶ collapses multiple subtypes into broader categories (e.g., 10+ epithelial subtypes into a single "epithelial cell" with 9,537 cells; fibroblast at 88% cardiac tissue versus the primary analysis's separate "fibroblast of cardiac tissue"); (2) tissue composition bias — CellHint¹⁶ neutrophils are liver-only (tissue-resident phenotype) versus multi-tissue in the primary analysis, and CellHint¹⁶ plasma cells are 89% intestinal (IgA-secreting); (3) tissue count correlation with rank reversal magnitude (Spearman rho = -0.526, p = 0.044), where types drawn from fewer tissues show the largest reversals. The global CellHint¹⁶ Procrustes signal is atlas-robust (obs/null = 0.448, p = 0.0001, n = 15), but per-type residual ranking is sensitive to centroid definition — an expected property of any centroid-based metric.
